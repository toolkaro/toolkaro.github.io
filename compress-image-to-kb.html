Compress Image to an Exact KB Size

For job forms, exam forms and online applications that demand a photo under 50KB, 100KB or 200KB.

Why forms ask for a photo under a fixed KB size

Government job portals, exam boards and university admission forms accept photos only below a fixed file size — commonly 20KB, 50KB, 100KB or 200KB. These limits exist because application portals store millions of photos and need every file small. A normal phone photo is 2–5 MB — twenty to a hundred times too big — so the portal rejects it, often with a confusing error message. This tool reduces your photo to exactly the size the form asks for, in one step.

How to compress your photo to an exact size

  1. Upload your photo (JPG or PNG).
  2. Choose the target size from the list, or pick "Custom…" and type any size in KB.
  3. Press the button — the tool adjusts quality and dimensions automatically until the file fits, keeping it as clear as possible.
  4. Download the ready photo and upload it to your form.

How the compression works

A photo file has two things that decide its size in KB: its dimensions (how many pixels wide and tall it is) and its quality setting (how much fine detail the JPG format keeps). This tool works like a careful human editor would. It first tries to keep your photo at full size and lowers the quality in small steps, testing after each step whether the file now fits the target. If quality alone cannot get the file small enough without wrecking it, the tool also reduces the dimensions gradually and tries again. The result is the clearest version of your photo that still lands at — or just under — the KB target you chose. That balancing act is why an exact-size tool gives a better-looking result than simply dragging a quality slider down once.

A worked example

Say your phone photo is 3.2 MB (about 3,277 KB) and an exam form demands a photo under 100KB. You select the photo, choose 100 KB and press the button. The tool might first save the full-size photo at reduced quality and get, say, 240 KB — still too big — so it shrinks the dimensions a little and tests again, settling on a version at about 98 KB that still looks sharp on screen. You download that file and the form accepts it, because the portal checks the file size, not the camera it came from.

Common mistakes that get photos rejected

  • Guessing with a screenshot. Screenshotting a photo does make it smaller, but the size is random, the quality drops badly, and some portals reject screenshots for looking unprofessional.
  • Making it too small. If the form says "under 200KB", a 15KB photo is unnecessarily blurry. Aim close to the limit the form gives you — the tool targets it automatically.
  • Wrong format. Most forms want JPG. PNG screenshots of photos are far larger; this tool always outputs JPG.
  • Forgetting the signature. Many forms ask for a signature image under a separate, smaller limit (often 10–20KB). The Custom size option handles that too.

Your photo stays private

Compression happens 100% inside your browser, using your own device. Your photo is never uploaded to any server, never stored and never shared — important when the photo is of you, your signature or your documents.

Exact KB or general compression — which tool do you need?

The two image tools on this site solve different problems, and picking the right one saves repeated attempts. Use this tool when a form, portal or person has given you a number: "under 50KB", "max 200KB", "between 20KB and 50KB". It hunts for that exact size automatically. Use the Image Compressor instead when there is no fixed target and you simply want a photo smaller — for WhatsApp, email, a website or phone storage — choosing the quality trade-off yourself with a slider. A quality percentage does not predict a final size (60% gives 140KB from one photo and 45KB from another), which is why exact-size jobs belong here. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What if my form needs a size that is not listed?

Choose "Custom…" and type any size in KB — for example 30, 75 or 150. The tool works to that exact target.

Q: Will the photo become blurry?

The tool keeps the highest quality that still fits your target size. For very small targets like 20KB there is some softness, which forms accept because they only check the file size and that the photo is recognisable.

Q: Can I compress a PNG?

Yes. PNG photos are accepted and converted to a compressed JPG, which is what most forms require.

Q: Is my photo uploaded to a server?

Never. Everything runs in your own browser, on your own device.

Q: Why does the result sometimes land just under my target?

Form limits are maximums — "under 100KB" means 100KB or less. The tool aims for the largest, clearest file that stays safely under the limit, so the portal never rejects it for being a fraction over.

Q: Can I compress a signature or document image?

Yes. Signatures for forms often need to be very small (10–20KB) — use the Custom option. For documents, keep in mind that heavy compression can blur small text, so choose the largest size your form allows.
