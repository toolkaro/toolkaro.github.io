The official passport photo size

Most countries and form portals use the 35mm × 45mm size (about 1.38 × 1.77 inches). It is the standard for passports in Pakistan, India and most of Asia and Europe, and for countless ID cards, visa forms and job applications. The United States uses a different square 2×2 inch size, so always check the instructions of the specific form you are filling — if it says 35×45mm, this tool is set to exactly that.

How to make your passport photo

  1. Upload a clear front-facing photo, ideally taken against a plain light wall in good, even light.
  2. Use Zoom so your face fills the frame, then use the move sliders to centre your head and shoulders.
  3. Download — the photo is saved at print quality (413 × 531 pixels at 300 DPI), ready for online forms and for printing.

What a good passport photo looks like

Official rules vary slightly by country, but the safe pattern is the same almost everywhere: face the camera directly with a neutral expression, both eyes open and visible, no sunglasses and no cap or hat (religious head coverings are normally allowed). Your head and shoulders should fill most of the frame, with a small gap above your head. Shadows across the face and red-eye from a flash are the two most common reasons real passport offices send people away — natural light from a window, facing you, avoids both.

A worked example

Say you have a selfie that is 3,024 × 4,032 pixels — tall, with lots of background above and beside you. Uploaded here, the tool crops it to the 35:45 ratio. You zoom to about 140% so your face is large, nudge the picture down slightly so there is a small space above your head, and download. The result is a 413 × 531 pixel photo — exactly the 35×45mm shape at print resolution — that a studio would charge for, made free in under a minute.

Printing a sheet of photos at home or a studio

One downloaded photo is a single 35×45mm print. If a shop prints it on a standard 4×6 inch sheet, ask them to place as many copies as fit (usually 8) — or simply hand them the file: every photo studio knows this size. At 300 DPI the file is sharp enough for a clean print, and because the background of the canvas is white, copies line up neatly on the sheet.

Common mistakes that get application photos rejected

  • Wrong proportions. Uploading a square or full-length photo where 35×45mm is demanded — the portal or office rejects it or crops it badly.
  • Face too small. A photo where your face is a tiny part of the frame fails the "head and shoulders" rule. Zoom in.
  • Busy background. This tool cannot remove a cluttered room behind you. Take the original against a plain light wall.
  • Old or filtered photos. Heavy beauty filters and very old photos can be refused because you must look like the photo. Use a recent, unfiltered picture.

Photo sizes around the world — know before you apply

The 35×45mm size this tool makes is the most common on earth: it is the standard for passports in Pakistan, India and most of Asia and Europe, and for Schengen visa applications. But it is not the only size in use, and sending the wrong shape is a quiet cause of rejected applications. The United States uses a square 2 × 2 inch (51 × 51mm) photo. China's visa photo is 33 × 48mm. Canada's passport photo is larger at 50 × 70mm. The safe habit: before making any photo, read the exact line in your form or on the embassy's page that gives the size, and follow that — not a neighbour's memory of a different country's form. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What size is a passport photo?

35mm × 45mm in most countries, including Pakistan and India. This tool crops to exactly that ratio and exports at 300 DPI print quality (413 × 531 pixels). Note the USA uses 2×2 inches instead — always follow your form's instructions.

Q: Can this tool change my background to white?

It places your photo on a white canvas, but it cannot remove a busy background from the photo itself. For official use, take your original photo against a plain light wall.

Q: My form also asks for under 50KB. What do I do?

Download the passport photo, then use our Compress Image to KB tool to set it to exactly 50KB or any size your form requires.

Q: Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything happens in your browser on your own device.

Q: Can I wear glasses in the photo?

Many countries now ask for glasses to be removed so eyes are fully visible and frames cast no shadow; some still allow them. Check your form's rules — when in doubt, take the photo without glasses.

Q: How recent must the photo be?

Most passport and visa rules require a photo taken within the last 6 months that looks like you now. A recent phone photo in good light is fine — you do not need a studio.
