Date Difference Calculator — days between dates

Deadlines, visa validity, project timelines — counted exactly.

How it counts

The result shows the full span in years, months and days (the way people naturally read it) and also the exact total in days and weeks — the way forms and contracts count it. The day total is exact: every calendar day between the two dates is counted, including leap days. The years/months/days figure is worked out the same way official age calculations are: complete years first, then complete calendar months, then the remaining days, always allowing for the real lengths of the months involved. The two answers always agree — they are the same span expressed two ways. (The "total months" line is marked approximate because a month is not a fixed number of days; it divides the day total by the average month length of 30.44 days.)

A worked example

From 1 March 2026 to 15 November 2026: count the full months first — March to November is 8 full months, ending on 1 November — then 14 more days to the 15th. The span reads 0 years, 8 months, 14 days, and the exact total is 259 days (37 weeks exactly). A human counting on a calendar often lands one or two days out because March, May, August and October have 31 days — exactly the kind of error this calculator removes.

Counting working days and deadlines

Contracts and offices often speak in working days, which exclude weekends. This calculator counts calendar days — the standard for visas, courts and most forms. To estimate working days from the result, a rough rule is that every 7 calendar days contain about 5 working days, so multiply the day total by 5/7 — but always check the exact wording of your document, because some deadlines count the start day itself and public holidays vary by country.

Common mistakes

  • The off-by-one error. "From Monday to Friday" is 4 full days of difference, though it touches 5 calendar dates. Forms that say "within 30 days of 1 March" mean the difference, not an inclusive count — this calculator gives the difference, the standard legal reading.
  • Assuming 30-day months. Hand counts over several months drift by several days because real months vary.
  • Forgetting 29 February. A span crossing a leap year contains one extra day — over a few years, manual counts that ignore it end up noticeably wrong.
  • Mixing date formats. 03/04 means 3 April in most of the world but March 4 in the US. The date pickers here remove that ambiguity entirely.

Counting days for real deadlines — two examples

A visa's validity window. Suppose a visa is issued on 1 February and is "valid for entry within 180 days". Put 1 February as the start date and count forward: 180 days later is 31 July, so your entry must happen on or before that date — and note that validity for entry and the permitted length of stay are two different numbers on most visas; count each one separately, from its own start date. Travellers who do this arithmetic once, at booking time, do not discover an expired window at the airport. A notice period. A contract says either side may end it with "30 days' notice". If notice is given on 15 April, the difference to 15 May is exactly 30 days — the period ends on 15 May under the standard difference-counting this calculator uses. But read the exact clause: if it says "30 clear days", both the day notice is given and the final day may be excluded, pushing the end to 16 or 17 May. For anything with legal or financial consequences, the document's wording rules — the calculator gives you the exact day counts to apply that wording to.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does it include both the start and end day?

The count is the exact number of full days between the two dates — the standard method used by forms and contracts. For example, 1 March to 8 March is 7 days, even though 8 calendar dates are touched.

Q: What if I enter the dates in the wrong order?

No problem — the calculator automatically treats the earlier date as the start.

Q: Can I count days until a future date?

Yes. Put today as the start date and the future date as the end date.

Q: Is it free?

Yes, free and unlimited.

Q: Does it count working days only?

No — it counts calendar days, which is what visas, contracts and most deadlines use. As a rough guide, about 5 of every 7 calendar days are working days, but check your document's exact wording for anything official.

Q: Why is "total months" marked approximate?

Because months have different lengths (28–31 days), there is no exact month total for an arbitrary span. The years/months/days answer on the same result is exact; the months figure is a convenience estimate using the average month of 30.44 days.
