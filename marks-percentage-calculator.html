Marks Percentage Calculator — for students

Enter each subject's marks and get your total, percentage and grade in one click.

How percentage is calculated

Percentage = (Total marks obtained ÷ Total maximum marks) × 100. This calculator adds all your subjects together and applies exactly that formula. Note that the subjects are combined first and the division happens once, at the end. That is the correct official method — and it is not the same as working out each subject's percentage and then averaging those percentages, which gives a wrong answer whenever subjects have different maximum marks (for example, a 100-mark paper and a 50-mark practical).

A worked example

Suppose a student scores: English 78/100, Mathematics 92/100, Physics 64/75, Chemistry 70/75 and Computer Science 88/100. The totals are 392 obtained out of 450 maximum. Percentage = 392 ÷ 450 × 100 = 87.11% — an A grade on the common scale. Notice how Physics and Chemistry (75-mark papers) pull correctly in the combined method: averaging the five subject percentages instead would give 87.33%, a small but real difference — in either direction depending on where the strong marks sit — that can matter at a merit-list cut-off.

Grading scale used

90% and above: A+ · 80–89%: A · 70–79%: B · 60–69%: C · 50–59%: D · 40–49%: E · Below 40%: F. Your board or university may use a slightly different scale — many Pakistani boards, for instance, treat 80%+ as A+ — but the percentage itself is always calculated the same way, and forms almost always ask for the percentage, not the grade.

Useful for

Checking results the day they are announced, filling admission forms that ask for your percentage, converting marks when applying for jobs or scholarships, and working out what you still need: for example, if a scholarship needs 80% and your first-year total is short, you can calculate exactly how many marks the remaining papers must produce.

Common mistakes students make

  • Averaging percentages. As the example shows, this is wrong when papers have different totals. Always add marks first, then divide once.
  • Forgetting a subject's real maximum. Practical papers, sessional marks and part-papers often have totals of 25, 50 or 75 — entering 100 for everything inflates or deflates the result.
  • Rounding too early. Keep full totals and round only the final percentage, ideally to two decimal places for forms.
  • Mixing two exams. Matric and inter (or two semesters) are separate results; admission forms usually want each percentage separately, not one merged number.

Percentage vs CGPA

Universities often report a CGPA instead of marks. There is no single universal CGPA-to-percentage formula: each university publishes its own official conversion table, and different scales (4.0 in Pakistan, 10 in India) convert differently. For anything official — admissions, equivalence certificates, job applications — use your own university's published table. This calculator is for marks-based results, where the percentage is exact and needs no conversion.

Planning backwards: the marks you still need

The most valuable use of a percentage is before the exam, not after it. Work backwards from your target: multiply the total maximum marks by the target percentage to get the total you must reach, then subtract the marks already banked. Example: a course has 600 maximum marks in total and you want 75% — that is 450 marks. If finished papers have given you 210, the remaining papers (worth 300) must produce 240, which is an 80% pace on what is left. That single calculation tells you whether your target is safe, at risk, or needs a different plan — while there is still time to act on it. A related warning for merit lists: scholarships and admissions are often decided within a band of half a percent, so always keep the full totals and round only the final answer, ideally to two decimal places, exactly as the example above does. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How do I calculate my percentage from marks?

Add all the marks you obtained, divide by the total maximum marks of all subjects, and multiply by 100. This calculator does it for you automatically.

Q: Can I use it for one subject only?

Yes — just fill in one row. It also works for a single test, quiz or assignment.

Q: What grade is 75%?

On the common scale used here, 70–79% is a B grade. Your own board may grade differently, but your percentage stays the same.

Q: Is this calculator free?

Yes, completely free and unlimited.

Q: My subjects have different total marks. Does that matter?

Yes, and this calculator handles it correctly: all obtained marks and all maximum marks are added separately first, then divided once. Do not average the per-subject percentages — that gives a wrong result when totals differ.

Q: How do I know how many marks I need for a target percentage?

Multiply the total maximum marks by the target (for 80%, multiply by 0.80) — that is the total you must reach. Subtract the marks you already have to see what the remaining papers must produce.

Q: My result card shows a different percentage. Who is right?

Recheck two things first: that every subject is included, and that each subject's real maximum was used — practical and sessional components are the usual cause of a mismatch. If your recalculation still differs from the card, contact your board or university promptly; result corrections are normally only accepted within a set window after announcement.
